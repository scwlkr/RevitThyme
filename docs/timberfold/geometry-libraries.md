# TimberFold geometry-library research

Researched 2026-10-19 against first-party documentation, tagged source and actual NuGet package contents. This is a recommendation for the approved planar exterior-shell scope, not implementation or measured qualification. No project dependencies, preserved baseline files or Revit sessions were changed.

## Recommendation

Use **Clipper2 2.0.0** for planar booleans/offsets and **LibTessDotNet 1.1.15's double assembly** for a qualified triangulation adapter. Keep custom 3D work limited to intersection/volume of convex triangular prisms and planar through-thickness trimming. Use **IxMilia.Dxf 0.8.4** behind a narrow DXF adapter. These packages provide a managed path; package compatibility with .NET Standard 2.0 makes them consumable by .NET 10, but does not establish correct behavior inside Revit. [Microsoft compatibility rules](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dotnet/standard/net-standard), [Clipper2 package](https://www.nuget.org/packages/Clipper2/2.0.0), [LibTessDotNet package](https://www.nuget.org/packages/LibTessDotNet/1.1.15), [IxMilia package](https://www.nuget.org/packages/IxMilia.Dxf/0.8.4).

Clipper2 2.0.0 also includes managed constrained Delaunay triangulation. Evaluate it against the same hole/concavity corpus before committing to LibTessDotNet: passing both numerical and topology gates could eliminate one dependency. Its documented triangulation input must have no intersecting/self-intersecting contours; normalization is required. Do not silently switch triangulators during a run because their output ordering and subdivisions can differ. [Official triangulation API](https://www.angusj.com/clipper2/Docs/Units/Clipper/Functions/Triangulate.htm).

## Numerical policy to qualify

The following are engineering proposals, not library guarantees:

- Convert Revit feet to physical model inches once (`feet * 12 / scaleDenominator`) and subtract a stable local origin before geometric work. Keep local-to-assembly transforms and source provenance separately. Do not multiply large georeferenced coordinates directly into the integer grid.
- Start with `Clipper64` at **100,000,000 units/in**: grid step `1e-8 in`, input rounding at most `5e-9 in` per coordinate. This leaves substantial room under the `1e-5 in` contour gate; it does not bound the accumulated error of every Boolean/offset/transform. Record scale and operation counts and measure final deviation. ClipperD also converts through integer coordinates internally. The author warns that intersection accuracy degrades above about `1e15` coordinate units, range checks are the caller's responsibility and tiny polygons may be discarded by rounding. Enforce an envelope comfortably below that range. [Coordinate range](https://www.angusj.com/clipper2/Docs/Overview.htm), [rounding behavior](https://www.angusj.com/clipper2/Docs/Robustness.htm).
- Preserve canonical outer/hole contours independently of render triangles. A tessellator's ability to repair complex inputs must not conceal extraction errors or missing glazing. Reject nonfinite, self-intersecting, collapsed, unaccounted or unexpectedly disconnected results.
- Treat the accepted **0.001 in arc chord tolerance** separately from the **1e-5 in straight-edge/export gate**. Apply independent arc-to-source checks and export-to-canonical-contour checks. A tessellated arc cannot generally satisfy the tighter gate against its original analytic curve.
- Never reuse a length epsilon as an area or volume epsilon. A displacement of `1e-5 in` over `100 in²` can change volume by approximately `1e-3 in³`, much greater than the **1e-6 in³** collision gate. A computed volume below the gate is insufficient when its numerical uncertainty crosses the gate. Use a qualified error bound/interval and fail unresolved cases; analytic/exact-rational small-case oracles can establish regression references.

## Bounded 3D collision and trimming

Triangulate each filled planar region, excluding its holes, then extrude each triangle through the part's physical thickness and assembly frame. This decomposes a concave perforated panel into convex triangular prisms with disjoint interiors. After broad-phase bounds filtering, intersect prism pairs by clipping against face half-spaces; calculate volume from a consistently oriented closed boundary. Sum disjoint cell-intersection volumes per original part pair before applying the collision gate, so many individually small overlaps cannot escape it. Convex polyhedron intersection through successive plane clipping is established in the authors' conservative remeshing research; it supports the approach, not a blanket numerical guarantee for a new C# implementation. [Authors' paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1412.4941), [Eberly's mesh-plane clipping treatment](https://www.geometrictools.com/Documentation/ClipMesh.pdf).

**Collision detection is separate from fabrication trimming.** Arbitrary prism subtraction can produce bevels or a thickness that varies across a panel. That result cannot be represented by one 2D laser contour. For the approved plain butt-joint scope, derive deterministic through-thickness planar trims from explicit joint ownership, project/clip them in the receiving part's frame, regenerate its prism, then rerun collision/completeness/web checks. For an obstruction only partway through the thickness, either use a documented conservative full-thickness trim that passes coverage rules or stop with source IDs and the failed joint condition. Never report a general solid difference as a valid flat part merely because its collision volume is zero.

## Deployment and qualification required

1. Pin package versions and hashes, retain full notices, and include transitive dependency notices. Use the `Double` assembly explicitly. Build/install on clean Windows x64 with no Python/pyRevit or development package cache; verify Revit's load context alongside other add-ins. Microsoft framework compatibility is preliminary evidence only.
2. Qualify holes, nested rings, multiple disconnected regions, concave outlines, coincident/collinear vertices, acute corners, tiny slivers, repeated offsets, large translations, tilted roof frames and normal reversal. Compare polygon coverage and area independently of the chosen triangulator; bounds and vertex counts alone do not establish equivalence.
3. Test prism collisions for coplanar contact, edge/vertex contact, parallel and nearly parallel slabs, wall/roof and roof/roof intersections, holes, clearance, and volumes just below/at/above `1e-6 in³`. Compare known analytic cases and an independently implemented oracle; report the uncertainty policy.
4. Write DXF with physical coordinates, explicit inch units and closed planar contours. Autodesk defines LWPOLYLINE flag bit 1 as closed and `$INSUNITS=1` as inches. Read back through an independent parser and actual native CAD; check units, `Z=0`, holes, 1-inch calibration geometry and maximum contour deviation. Writer/reader agreement in the same library is insufficient. [Autodesk polyline definition](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2020/ENU/AutoCAD-DXF/files/GUID-748FC305-F3F2-4F74-825A-61F04D757A50.htm), [Autodesk DXF reference](https://images.autodesk.com/adsk/files/acad_dxf.pdf).
5. Benchmark extraction/computation/output separately on the four original gable, irregular/intersecting, multistorey and flat-roof RVTs. Check repeatability and source preservation in pinned isolated Revit copies. Record physical cutting, kerf, measured thickness, web strength and assembly checks as outstanding until performed by a person.
